# Preliminary Investment Insights from Ghana’s 2025 Energy Baseline

## Purpose

This document interprets Ghana’s 2025 energy indicators from an investment perspective. The findings are preliminary and will be tested using tariff, technology-cost, financing, regulatory and customer-level data.

## Key Investment Signals

| Indicator | 2025 Result | Preliminary Investment Relevance |
|---|---:|---|
| Thermal share of electricity generation | 68% | Indicates substantial dependence on fuel-based generation |
| Other renewable generation share | 0.71% | Suggests significant room for solar and other renewable-energy expansion |
| Other renewable installed capacity | 183 MW | Shows that non-hydro renewable capacity remains limited |
| Industrial electricity consumption | 8,636 GWh | Demonstrates a substantial industrial electricity market |
| Industry’s share of electricity consumption | Approximately 39.6% | Supports investigation of industrial energy-efficiency and commercial solar opportunities |
| System peak load | 4,283 MW | Indicates substantial and growing electricity demand |
| Population electricity access rate | 89.13% | Shows strong national access but a remaining underserved population |
| Household electricity access rate | 87.99% | Suggests potential for distributed energy solutions in underserved areas |

## 1. Solar-Power Opportunity

Non-hydro renewable sources generated only 197 GWh, equivalent to 0.71% of Ghana’s total electricity generation in 2025.

This indicates potential room for:

- Commercial and industrial rooftop solar
- Solar-plus-battery systems
- Distributed solar
- Solar mini-grids
- Solar systems for schools, hospitals and other public institutions

However, the low renewable share alone does not prove that every solar investment will be financially viable. Further analysis is required on electricity tariffs, solar-system costs, financing conditions, customer demand, grid requirements and payback periods.

## 2. Industrial Energy-Efficiency Opportunity

Ghanaian industries consumed 8,636 GWh of electricity in 2025. This represented approximately 39.6% of the country’s total electricity consumption.

This suggests a potentially significant market for:

- Energy-efficient motors
- Industrial cooling and refrigeration
- Efficient boilers and process heating
- Energy-monitoring systems
- Power-factor correction
- Building-energy management systems
- Waste-heat recovery
- Energy-service company contracts

The next stage should identify which Ghanaian industries have the highest energy costs and strongest ability to invest.

## 3. Energy-Security and Cost-Risk Opportunity

Thermal generation accounted for 68% of Ghana’s electricity generation in 2025.

High dependence on thermal generation may expose the electricity system to:

- Fuel-price volatility
- Foreign-exchange risk
- Natural-gas supply constraints
- Petroleum import costs
- Power-generation cost increases

Solar power, battery storage and energy efficiency could help reduce some of these risks. Their actual economic value must be tested through financial and technical analysis.

## 4. Distributed-Energy Opportunity

Ghana’s population electricity-access rate reached 89.13% in 2025, while household access reached 87.99%.

This means that an access gap remains, particularly in underserved locations. Possible solutions include:

- Solar home systems
- Community mini-grids
- Solar systems for productive agricultural use
- Solar-powered cold storage
- Solar systems for rural schools and health facilities

Affordability, customer density, payment capacity and existing electrification plans must be considered before identifying investable projects.

## Preliminary Opportunity Priorities

### Higher-priority opportunities for further investigation

1. Commercial and industrial rooftop solar
2. Industrial energy-efficiency services
3. Solar-plus-battery systems for businesses
4. Equipment leasing and performance-based energy financing

### Opportunities requiring additional location-specific research

1. Rural mini-grids
2. Solar home systems
3. Public-institution solar projects
4. Agricultural productive-use solar systems

## Important Limitation

These findings represent opportunity signals rather than final investment recommendations. A complete investment assessment must examine:

- Project capital costs
- Electricity tariffs
- Financing costs
- Customer creditworthiness
- Foreign-exchange exposure
- Regulatory requirements
- Grid-connection procedures
- Revenue models
- Payback periods
- Environmental and social risks
