# Ghana Energy Transition Investment Analytics

## Solar Power, Industrial Energy Efficiency and Green Finance

> Africa’s energy transition is not only an environmental issue. It is an investment, industrial-productivity and development opportunity.

## Project Overview

This project applies data science, economic analysis and investment assessment to Ghana’s energy transition.

It combines Python, Power BI, energy-market data, electricity tariffs, financial analysis and policy research to identify potentially investable opportunities in:

- Solar power
- Industrial energy efficiency
- Green finance
- Energy infrastructure
- Distributed energy systems

The project will produce both an interactive analytics dashboard and a professional Ghana Energy Transition Investment Brief.

## Core Research Question

Where are the most investable opportunities in Ghana’s solar-power and industrial energy-efficiency markets, and how can data analysis and green finance help mobilize capital for them?

## Project Objectives

1. Build a clean and documented Ghana energy dataset.
2. Analyse electricity supply, generation, demand and consumption.
3. Examine electricity tariffs across customer categories.
4. Identify commercially relevant solar-power opportunities.
5. Assess industrial energy-efficiency opportunities.
6. Develop basic solar financial and payback models.
7. Evaluate green-finance instruments and investment risks.
8. Build an interactive Power BI dashboard.
9. Produce a professional investment brief.
10. Communicate the findings through charts and LinkedIn content.

## Preliminary findings

The illustrative 100 kWp Ghana solar scenario produces:
- Simple payback: 3.17 years.
- Twenty-year NPV: GHS 2,083,189.03.
- NPV including an illustrative year-10 replacement:
  GHS 2,010,246.47.
- Break-even self-consumption including replacement: 34.97%
  at the base installation cost.

Results depend on documented inputs and illustrative assumptions.
One tested higher-cost, lower-self-consumption case becomes
negative after including replacement costs.

The Ghana–South Africa comparison documents rooftop adoption,
supplier pricing, advertised financing, connection requirements
and a commercial tariff example. It does not establish what caused
adoption differences or which country offers better returns.

Next evidence priorities are scoped Ghana installation quotations,
customer demand profiles and complete financing terms.
### Ghana base-case assumptions

| Input | Value |
|---|---:|
| System capacity | 100 kWp |
| Illustrative installation cost | USD 900/kWp |
| Dated exchange-rate input | GHS 11.6650/USD |
| Initial investment | GHS 1,049,850 |
| On-site self-consumption | 90% |
| Annual maintenance | 1.5% of initial investment |
| Annual generation degradation | 0.5% |
| Project lifetime | 20 years |
| Illustrative real GHS discount rate | 8% |
| Export value | Zero |
| Replacement stress test | 15% of initial investment at year 10 |

Cash flows are expressed in constant GHS. Tariff and maintenance
costs remain constant in real terms. Simple payback uses first-year
net savings; NPV incorporates generation degradation.

This is a preliminary, unlevered scenario. Customer-specific costs,
tax treatment, financing and operating conditions require verification.
See notebook 05 and its source references for details.
